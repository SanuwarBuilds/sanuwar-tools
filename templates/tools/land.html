Land measurement in India

India uses a mix of standard and customary land units. Square feet, square metres, acres and hectares are standard everywhere. Bigha, Katha, Biswa, Kanal, Marla, Guntha, Lessa and similar names are local units, and their size changes from state to state — sometimes from district to district. There is no single national Bigha.

This calculator keeps the local standard separate from the unit name. Choose your state first, then choose the local standard that matches your land records, and convert between that standard and standard units.

Assam: Bigha, Katha and Lessa

In the Brahmaputra Valley standard used here, 1 Bigha = 14,400 sq ft, 1 Katha = 2,880 sq ft, 1 Lessa = 144 sq ft, and 1 Pura = 57,600 sq ft. One Bigha is 5 Katha, and one Katha is 20 Lessa.

The Barak Valley / Cachar reference uses the same Bigha size but divides it into 20 Katha of 720 sq ft, with 16 Chatak in a Katha. Always check the standard written in the local patta or revenue record before relying on a number.

Why the same unit changes size

Customary units were defined by local revenue practice. A Bihar Bigha is commonly cited between 27,225 and 65,340 sq ft depending on the district. Punjab and Haryana use Killa, Kanal, Marla and Sarsahi, where 1 Killa = 8 Kanal and 1 Kanal = 20 Marla. Gujarat has both 16-Guntha and 24-Guntha Vigha references. The calculator shows the selected standard, its source value, and a warning whenever the value is a regional reference rather than a universal rule.

How this calculator works

Enter the area, choose the unit you have and the unit you want. The result appears immediately with an exact secondary line in square feet, square metres and acres, plus a local breakdown such as 2 Bigha · 2 Katha · 10 Lessa. Language, state, standard, units, area and precision are stored in the page URL, so you can bookmark or share a conversion.

Methodology and sources

The calculator keeps a small, auditable registry instead of assuming one national value for each local unit. Every standard records its unit relationships, square-foot value and a plain-language note about where it applies.

  • State revenue practice: entries follow the customary relationships used in state or regional land records, such as Bigha–Katha–Lessa in Assam or Killa–Kanal–Marla in Punjab and Haryana.
  • Common public references: values are cross-checked against multiple commonly cited sources. Where those sources disagree, the note gives the range and the calculator uses one clearly stated value.
  • Local variation: district, tehsil and village practice can differ. Selecting a state narrows the reference; it cannot certify the area of a particular plot.
  • Corrections: if a local standard differs, send the district, unit relationship and a public record or source through the contact page. Corrections are reviewed before the registry changes.

Accuracy note

Revenue offices, registered deeds and licensed surveyors always take precedence over any online conversion. Read the methodology and sources.

Important

Land measurement names and values differ by state, district and village, and sometimes between nearby circles. The values shown here are commonly cited reference conversions, not a legal survey. Always confirm the area with your official land records, registered deed, or a licensed surveyor before making a purchase, sale, loan or construction decision. This calculator does not provide legal, financial or valuation advice.
